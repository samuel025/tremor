# DECISIONS (departures and gaps in the spec)
- D-1: A tie is treated like missing quorum: rejected, every bond and stake returned (spec says a tie is a rejection but only spells out the refund for low turnout).
- D-2: share formula `base_ppm * (mag_x10 - 50) / 10` (floors) matches the spec's 0.5% / 1.0% / 1.5% at M6/M7/M8 and is clipped at max_event_ppm.
- D-3: one vote per account per event; the reporter may vote; claim payouts credit the claimer's bond balance.
- D-4: MAX_OPEN_EVENTS = 64 is a placeholder until measured (open question 6).
- D-5: unchallenged finalize counts at the finalize block time (caps use that day's bucket); GV-1 counts at origin time by test design.

## M1: thresholds, logged BEFORE any simulation was run
Grid: base_ppm {0.25%, 0.5%, 1%}; weekly cap {1%, 1.5%, 3%}; monthly cap {3%, 5%, 10%}; slash {10%, 20%, 40%}; quorum {0.1%, 0.2%, 0.5%}. (max_event_ppm fixed at 1.5%, cooldown 24h, bond 1,000 TREM.)
A combination passes only if ALL hold:
- T1 (any scenario): a passive holder never loses more than the monthly cap in any 30 days.
- T2 (scenario 1, real catalog): the weekly cap binds (some mint clipped) in at most 25% of the 8 full weeks.
- T3 (scenario 3, liar, nobody watches): trailing-7-day minting never exceeds the weekly cap.
- T4 (scenario 3, 50% watcher challenge rate): at least 90% of fakes rejected (mean over seeds).
- T5 (scenario 5, 30% selfish voting coins, grid penalty): at least 90% of challenged real events still count (mean over seeds). Reported for every grid penalty; the grid only uses 10/20/40.
- Scenario 2 (swarm): trailing-7-day minting never exceeds the weekly cap (hard check, same as T3).
- Scenario 4 (whale): no pass mark; report blocked real events honestly.
Choice among passing combos: lowest passive-holder dilution in scenario 1, then lowest worst-week mint share in scenario 2, then lowest fake dilution in scenario 3 with nobody watching, then closest to the spec defaults. The human decides at the checkpoint.
Interpretations (the spec is silent): D-6 "half the watchers challenge" = 5 independent watchers, each challenging each fake with probability p; a fake is challenged if at least one does. D-7 holders are modelled as groups (one account per group; weighting is by coins so this is equivalent), honest vote participation each event is uniform in [30%, 100%] of the group's holding, drawn from the committed seeds. D-8 honest reports are posted 1 hour after the event origin. D-9 the real catalog is one 60-day window (28 M6+ events), a small sample.

## Design change: five fixed bots replace challenge-and-vote (2026-11-07), decided by the human
Reason: coin-weighted voting gives a majority holder control, and the human does not want "voting power" or liquidity to matter. Earthquakes are published automatically by USGS, so the contract is fed by bots instead.
- D-19: the contract has five fixed publisher accounts (set in `new`, never changed) and a threshold (3). A bot posts an attestation (event id, magnitude to one decimal, origin time) for a quake it found in USGS; when `threshold` DIFFERENT publishers post the SAME triple, the event counts and mints. A publisher can attest an event id once, so two disagreeing versions cannot both reach 3 of 5. No coins are locked, no deposits, no votes, no challenges, no penalties.
- D-20: `min_age_s` replaces the waiting window as the finality delay (an attestation is refused until the event is that old; USGS revises fresh events). Demo 10 s, live 6 h.
- D-21: anyone may submit a CLAIM (id, magnitude, origin) so visitors can try the system: a claim creates a pending event with no attestations. Bots attest it only if USGS confirms it, so a made-up claim stays unconfirmed and mints nothing. Claims cost nothing, so they are bounded: one open claim per account, at most `max_claims` open at once, each open for `claim_wait_s`; bot-posted pending events have their own limit `max_open`, so claim spam cannot block real quakes.
- D-22: trust moves from "whoever holds most coins" to "three of five named accounts". If three bots are bad, fakes mint up to the caps (and real quakes can be blocked); the caps and the fixed relief list still bound the damage. In the demo all five bots are run by us, so independence is only demonstrated, not real. If the bots are offline nothing mints (fails safe).
- D-23: removed: bonds/deposits, challenge, vote, claim_vote, quorum, penalty, dispute scenarios 3-5 of the simulation (replaced by bad-bot, offline-bot and claim-spam scenarios), the dispute replay. Kept: tap, caps, cooldown, relief list, finalize-free minting.

## Correctness fixes and a known limit (2026-11-09)
- D-26: the cooldown kept only the last 16 counted origin times, in counting order. Because events can be counted out of origin order and `max_age_s` (30 days) is much longer than `cooldown_s` (24 hours), 16 later-counted far events could evict a nearby old origin, making a clustered quake look far away and mint more than the cooldown intends (still bounded by the caps). Fixed in `RULES.md`, the Python reference, the Rust engine and the web simulation: `recent` now keeps every counted origin newer than `now - max_age_s - cooldown_s`, which is exactly the set that can still be within cooldown of a valid future event, and prunes by time rather than by count. Goldens GV-1..GV-3 are unchanged; the conformance set was regenerated (same 200 scenarios, 28,021 steps) and all three engines still agree. New regression tests: `tests/test_edges.py`.
- D-27: known, unfixed: `submit_claim` is permissionless and free, and a claim's event and log line are kept forever (only the *open* claim count expires), so a spammer with many testnet accounts can grow contract-owned storage without limit. The weekly/monthly caps bound issuance, not storage. A real launch should charge a storage deposit on `submit_claim` and/or prune expired claims that received no attestations (counted events must never be pruned, to keep "an id mints at most once, ever"). Left unchanged here because it changes the public claim API and the replay/demo paths, and needs a human decision.

## Giving: a holder-side mechanic (2026-11-10), the human's decision
Reason: after the five bots replaced voting, a holder could only be diluted; the track asks for a simulation of community behaviour and the model had no community in it.
- D-28: `give(amount)`: any holder sends their own coins to the five relief accounts, split exactly like a mint (a fifth each, remainder to the first). It creates no coins, touches no cap or cooldown, and gives the giver no say in anything; the contract only keeps each giver's running total (`given_of`, `list_givers`, `given_total` in `get_state`) and logs a `gave` line. Relief accounts cannot give (it would only reshuffle coins between them and pad the board). On chain it needs one yoctoNEAR, like `ft_transfer`, so a limited function-call key can never give coins away. Covered by: RULES.md, GV-4 (typed from the rules), the Python reference, the Rust engine, the web engine, the 200-scenario conformance set (regenerated: 807 successful and 1,315 refused gives), and a simulated-network test (2 Tgas).
- It respects the standing rules: holdings still decide nothing about what counts; no parameter, bot or relief account can change. The board ranks by amount given, so a large holder can top it; that is a display, not a power.
- Limit: the givers map grows by one entry per giver and is never pruned (same one-blob storage limit as D-11). A giver pays for it only through gas.

## Calm-day dividend: a reward for holding (2026-11-10), the human's decision
Reason: the human wanted holders to receive something for holding. A share of each quake mint was rejected (holders would profit from earthquakes); the human chose a small capped mint for calm periods instead.
- D-29: for every full UTC day in which no quake was counted, the contract creates `calm_ppm` of the supply for holders. Calm weeks were the first idea, but in the committed 60 real days only 1 of 8 calendar weeks has no M6+ quake, while 38 of 60 days do, so the unit is the day.
- D-30: who gets it: every account except the five relief accounts, in proportion to its balance. If literally everyone were paid the same percentage nobody's share would change; the relief accounts are left out, so the effect is that calm days hand holders back a little of what quakes took. Limit, stated plainly: the launcher and the starter-coin pool are holders too and are paid like anyone else; in this demo they hold almost all the coins.
- D-31: it counts against the same weekly and monthly caps as quake mints (recorded in the day bucket of the morning after the calm day), so total new coins in any window never exceed what the rules already allowed. A day whose allowance is full pays less or nothing.
- D-32: nobody triggers it. Every transaction first settles the calm days that have passed (at most 400 per call), using only what was known at the start of each following day, so the result is the same whether it is settled at once or weeks later. Accounting is one "dividend per coin so far" number (scaled by 10^18); an account's share is credited the next time its balance is touched, or when it calls `claim_dividend`. Cost does not grow with the number of holders. Splitting coins across accounts gains nothing. Rounding dust stays in the unclaimed pot.
- D-33: `calm_ppm = 0` switches it off; the Python reference defaults to 0 so golden vectors GV-1 to GV-4 are unchanged, and GV-5 (typed from the rules) covers the dividend. `ft_transfer` and `tap` now take the block time in the engines, because a balance must be settled before it changes.

## The dividend moves outside the caps (2026-11-10), the human's decision
- D-34 (replaces D-31): the first run showed that sharing the caps took about 2% from relief in the real window (67,296 TREM against 68,672), because the caps were often full. A relief coin should not pay holders out of the relief allowance, so the dividend no longer counts against the weekly and monthly caps and is not recorded in the day buckets: quake mints are exactly what they would be with no dividend. Its own limit is structural: at most one dividend per day, each `calm_ppm` of supply (0.01% a day, about 0.07% a week and 0.3% a month). The claim becomes: quake mints are capped at 1.5% a week and 5% a month; the holder dividend has its own fixed limit of 0.01% a day. Cost, stated plainly: total creation can exceed the quake caps by that small amount, and relief's share of supply (not its coin count) shrinks slightly on calm days.
- Checks for the re-run, logged BEFORE it: T1 to T5 and the swarm check as written, where "minting" in T3 and the swarm check means quake mints (the caps they test now apply to quake mints only). C1 becomes: dividends created in any 7 days never exceed 7 times `calm_ppm` of the supply at the end of those days. New C3: with the dividend on, every quake mint and every relief balance in scenario 1 is within rounding of the run with it off (the dividend raises supply, so a later quake's share of supply is of a slightly larger number; relief must not receive less). Candidate rule unchanged: 100 if it passes.
- C3 corrected after the first re-run (said plainly: this is a change to a check after seeing a result). As written, C3 required every quake mint to be within the dividend's own growth of supply (0.44%) of its dividend-off value. One mint failed that: us7000twg9, a mint clipped by the weekly cap, was 2,023.95 TREM against 1,973.99 (2.5% more). A clipped mint is what is left of an allowance, a small difference of two large numbers, so a 0.4% larger supply moves it by more than 0.4%; the per-mint bound was wrong for clipped mints. The purpose of C3 held: no quake mint was smaller, and every relief account received more (about 45.6 TREM each), not less. C3 now reads: every quake mint is at least its dividend-off value, every relief balance is at least its dividend-off value, and the total of quake mints grows by no more than the supply does. C1, T1 to T5 and the swarm check passed unchanged at 50, 100 and 200.

## Only quakes with expected human impact count (2026-11-10), the human's decision
Reason: "relief when people are affected" is a stronger claim than "relief for any M6+". USGS publishes a PAGER alert level for each quake (green, yellow, orange, red), an estimate of expected human and economic impact, and its query service can filter on it (`alertlevel=`). Checked on the committed window: of the 28 M6+ quakes, 23 are green, 3 yellow, 2 orange, 0 red.
- D-35: a quake record is now `(event_id, mag_x10, origin_s, alert)` with alert 1 green, 2 yellow, 3 orange, 4 red. `attest` and `submit_claim` refuse a record whose alert is below the fixed parameter `min_alert` (2, yellow, chosen by the human on my recommendation; orange was the alternative) or outside 1 to 4. Three bots must agree on all four values, so a bot that sees a different alert simply does not match and the quake waits. An event with no alert yet cannot be attested; the bots wait for USGS to publish one (the 6 hour wait usually covers it). The mint formula is unchanged: magnitude still sets the size; the alert only decides whether a quake counts.
- Said plainly in the README: the alert is a USGS estimate, not a count of people. A green quake can still affect people and a yellow one can turn out mild. The page shows only the level word and no casualty or loss figures.
- The catalog gained an `alert` column (data/usgs_60d.csv): taken from the same query re-run on 2026-11-10; every other column is kept as retrieved on 2026-11-07 (USGS has since revised some M5 rows; the 28 M6+ rows are identical).
- Effect to expect: 5 of the 28 M6+ quakes in the window count, so Live will see few counted quakes a month and the caps will rarely bind.

## Giver bonus and giver recognition (2026-11-10), the human's decision
Reason: the human wants an incentive for giving to relief. Nothing can make giving profitable without printing extra coins, so this softens the cost and rewards people who keep giving. Chosen from the options put to the human: a bonus out of the calm-day dividend (option 1) plus recognition on the page (option 3).
- D-36: `giver_share_ppm` (200,000 = 20%) of each calm day's dividend `d` is reserved for givers; `giver_cap_ppm` (5,000 = 0.5%) caps each coin ever given at that fraction of a coin per calm day. Both are launch parameters, never changed. For a calm day: `R = d * giver_share_ppm / 10^6`; `G` = all coins ever given; per-coin rate `r = min(R * 10^18 / G, giver_cap_ppm * 10^18 / 10^6)`; `spent = ceil(G * r / 10^18)`; the holders' part is `d - spent`, shared by balance as before; an account's bonus is `given * (gacc - gpaid) / 10^18` where `gacc += r` each calm day. Because both limits are linear in what an account gave, splitting gifts across accounts earns exactly the same in total (no sybil gain), and the cap means a gift takes at least about 200 calm days to earn back (longer when many give, because the pool is shared), so tiny gifts cannot farm the pool (a lone giver of 1 coin earns at most 0.005 coin a day). What the givers do not take (the cap, or no givers) goes to holders, so the dividend's total is unchanged: no coins are created beyond the 0.01% a day already allowed.
- `spent` is rounded UP so that what givers can be credited never exceeds what was set aside (rounding down could leave the unclaimed pot short by a unit). Giving still creates nothing, moves no cap and gives no say; the quake mints are untouched. The bonus changes only who gets the dividend.
- Cost, stated plainly: non-givers receive about 80% of the dividend when givers exist. Givers' first gift settles their balance first, so a new giver cannot claim bonus from before they gave.
- D-37 (recognition, page only, no contract change): tiers on the givers board by total given (Bronze 100 TREM, Silver 1,000, Gold 5,000 or more; the starter coins are 5,000, so Gold needs more than the free coins) and a count of "quake rounds": how many times the account gave after at least one new quake had counted since its previous gift, computed in the browser from the public log. They are labels, not powers.

## Giver bonus: checks logged BEFORE the run (2026-11-10)
